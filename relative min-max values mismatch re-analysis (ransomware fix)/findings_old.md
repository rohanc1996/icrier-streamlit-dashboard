# CHIPS re-analysis: Relative scores after the Ransomware-data fix

Re-analysis of the dashboard's **Relative** CHIPS output against the scores
published in last year's report, re-run after the underlying Relative raw data
was corrected. Generated by `scripts/reconcile_chips.py` and
`scripts/divergence_detail.py` (unchanged code) against the updated files:

- Dashboard source: `data/SIDE 2026 - Relative.csv` (the dashboard's Relative
  base dataset), full-range min-max, Relative columns mapped onto the canonical
  CHIPS indicator set.
- Published source: `data/SIDE 2026 - AI augmented Relative Index 2026.csv`
  (CHIPS score line 96).

## What changed in the data

The Relative CSV's ransomware input column
(`Ransomware attacks detected per month as a % of internet users`) had been
rounded to 2 decimal places, so almost every value collapsed to `0.00` — only
**4 of 71** countries were non-zero. Under full-range min-max that column was
effectively constant, and the dashboard could not reproduce the published
Ransomware-attacks scores, which carry real spread.

The column now carries full precision (all 71 countries non-degenerate, range
~0.0000–0.0091 before scaling). The published file was only touched cosmetically
(a few indicator-name trailing spaces trimmed; the Uzbekistan VC-investment cell
blanked — the previously-noted linking error).

## Summary

- 71 countries reconciled; **65 match to within 0.1 points** (8 exactly).
- Only **4 real divergences** remain among countries that have a published score
  (largest: Italy 1.37, Switzerland 1.15, Uzbekistan 0.68, South Africa 0.22).
- 2 countries (Dominican Republic, Kuwait) still have no published CHIPS score
  but do on the dashboard — counted as diverging.

| Root cause | Countries |
|---|---|
| ROUNDING-ONLY | 65 |
| INDICATOR (data-version / min-max bound) | 4 |
| AGGREGATION / WEIGHTS | 2 |

## The Ransomware divergence is resolved

Before the fix, **25 of 28 flagged countries** traced back to the single
PROTECT · Risk of Attack indicator *Ransomware attacks (30-day average)*, with
large dashboard-vs-published gaps (e.g. Italy 75.8 vs 100.0, Switzerland 36.3 vs
0.0, Russian Federation 57.1 vs 100.0). After the fix, **no country is flagged
for Ransomware attacks**: the dashboard's full-min-max Ransomware score now
reproduces the published Relative index's Ransomware row almost exactly for
every country (rank correlation ≈ 1.0, max absolute gap ≈ 0.005 points).

## What the remaining divergence is driven by

- **Italy (1.37)** — CONNECT · Affordability: *Fixed (broadband) internet price,
  USD PPP* published 11.9 vs dashboard 97.0 (the same known published
  broadband-data version issue seen in the Absolute reconciliation), plus a
  small 0.7-pt *% of population that are using AI* bound drift.
- **Switzerland (1.15)** — two contributors: *% of population that are using AI*
  49.39 vs 50.0 (CONNECT · AI indicator-bound drift) and the INNOVATE · AI
  sub-pillar (published 31.8 vs dashboard 18.4). The dashboard aggregates that
  sub-pillar through two internal groups (research pair ½ + investment /
  commercial ½) while the published sheet uses a flat weighting — a structural
  aggregation difference, not a data error.
- **Uzbekistan (0.68)** — SUSTAINABILITY · Sustainability sub-pillar aggregates
  to 37.76 on the dashboard vs 32.2 published even though every shared indicator
  reconciles individually (composition / weighting residual; the published VC
  cell for Uzbekistan is now blank per the earlier linking-error note).
- **South Africa (0.22)** — *Renewable energy share of electricity
  production (%)*: dashboard 8.1 vs published −0.2 (data-version / negative
  outlier drift; small overall effect).
- **Dominican Republic / Kuwait** — no published CHIPS score to compare
  against; the dashboard scores them (CHIPS 32.4 → rank 35 and 47.3 → rank 3).

The Relative dashboard now tracks the published Relative index almost as closely
as the Absolute dashboard tracked the published Absolute index. What remains is a
short, explainable list: one known published-data issue (Italy broadband), the
structural INNOVATE · AI weighting difference (Switzerland), small
indicator-bound drifts, and two countries the published sheet simply does not
score.
